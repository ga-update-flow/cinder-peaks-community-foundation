Our history

{{ site.org.name }} was established in {{ site.org.founded }} by a group of Flagstaff families, business owners, and civic leaders who wanted a permanent, local source of charitable support for the region. The first fund, a $40,000 unrestricted gift, supported a community health clinic and a children's library program.

Today the Foundation stewards {{ site.stats[2].value }} charitable funds created by individuals, families, businesses, and nonprofits. Together, those funds have granted more than $94 million to community causes. The Foundation is an independent, publicly supported 501(c)(3) public charity governed by a volunteer board of directors drawn from across the region.

What we do

  • Grantmaking. We run nine grant programs that award about $2.4 million in competitive grants each year.
  • Charitable funds. We help donors create donor-advised, designated, field-of-interest, and scholarship funds, and we manage endowments for local nonprofits.
  • Nonprofit learning. Our Nonprofit Learning Hub offers workshops, peer cohorts, and practical guides.
  • Community leadership. We commission research, such as the Northern Arizona Infant Care Gap brief, and convene partners around shared challenges.

Our approach to community impact

We believe the people closest to a challenge are best positioned to solve it. That belief shapes how we work:

  • We favor unrestricted general operating support, because nonprofits know best where their dollars are needed.
  • Community advisory councils recommend grants for the Plateau Indigenous Communities Fund, the First Steps Fund, and the Rising Roots Fund.
  • We set award amounts by budget size so small rural organizations compete fairly with larger ones.
  • We keep applications and reports short, and we accept oral and video submissions for several programs.
