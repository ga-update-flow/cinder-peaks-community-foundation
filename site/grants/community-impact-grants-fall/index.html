Community Impact Grants: Fall Cycle

Unrestricted general operating support for nonprofits working in education and youth, arts and culture, environment and land stewardship, and animal welfare in northern Arizona.

Program description

The Fall Cycle of the Community Impact Grants program provides one-year, unrestricted general operating support to organizations that strengthen learning, creativity, and the natural environment across Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, and Apache counties.

Award amounts are based on the applicant's annual operating budget. The Foundation awarded $612,500 to 46 organizations in the Fall 2026 cycle and expects a similar level of funding in 2027.

Focus and impact areas

Education & YouthArts & CultureEnvironment & Land StewardshipAnimal Welfare

  • Early literacy, math, and school readiness
  • Out-of-school-time learning and mentoring
  • College and career pathways and opportunity youth re-engagement
  • Arts access for youth and families and preservation of cultural heritage
  • Watershed protection, forest restoration, and environmental justice
  • Local food systems and renewable energy
  • Spay and neuter access, humane education, and wildlife rehabilitation

Population served

  • Children and youth ages 5–18
  • Opportunity youth ages 16–24
  • Families in rural and tribal communities
  • Traditional artists and culture bearers
  • Rural pet owners

Eligibility

Geographic eligibility

Organizations must be located in or provide services to residents of Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, or Apache counties, Arizona.

Who may apply

  • Tax-exempt under Section 501(c)(3), a public or governmental agency (including public schools), a federally recognized tribe or tribal program, or a project with a qualified fiscal sponsor
  • Located in or primarily serving residents of Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, or Apache counties
  • In operation for at least two years with a board-approved annual budget
  • Non-discriminatory in hiring, board membership, and delivery of services
  • Work aligns with at least one Fall Cycle field of interest: Education & Youth, Arts & Culture, Environment & Land Stewardship, or Animal Welfare
  • One Community Impact Grant application per organization per calendar year (Spring or Fall, not both)
  • Fundraising events and sponsorships

What we do not fund

  • Organizations that received a Community Impact Grant in calendar year 2026
  • Individuals, scholarships, or private school tuition
  • Religious activities
  • Capital campaigns, endowments, or debt reduction
  • Lobbying or partisan political activity

How to apply

Applications are submitted through the CPCF Grant Portal during the open window. The Fall Cycle uses the same application worksheet as the Spring Cycle.

  1. Confirm eligibility. Review the 2027 Fall Cycle Guidelines and complete the Grant Eligibility Checklist.
  2. Prepare your answers. Download the Community Impact Grant Application Worksheet and draft your responses.
  3. Create or update your portal account. New applicants create an organization profile in the CPCF Grant Portal. Returning applicants confirm that their profile is current.
  4. Submit the application. Submit the online application, operating budget, and most recent financial statements by 5:00 p.m. MST on August 20, 2027.
  5. Site visit and decision. Program staff may schedule site visits in September and October. Decisions are announced by November 19, 2027.

General instructions for the CPCF Grant Portal are on the How to Apply page.

Review criteria

  • Alignment with Fall Cycle fields of interest
  • Evidence of community need and results
  • Equity: the organization is led by, or meaningfully involves, the communities it serves
  • Collaboration and partnership
  • Financial health and sustainability
  • Completeness of the application

Reporting requirements

Grantees submit a brief Community Impact Story Report at the end of the grant period.

  • A short narrative (300–500 words) or photo story describing how the grant strengthened the organization
  • Number of people served during the grant year
  • Permission indicating whether CPCF may share the story publicly

Due: January 31, 2029.

Reporting form: Community Impact Story Report Form (PDF)

Frequently asked questions

Are public schools eligible?

Yes. Public school districts, charter schools, and Bureau of Indian Education schools may apply. A school's award tier is based on the budget of the program or school site, not the full district budget.

Can we apply to both the Fall Cycle and the Plateau Indigenous Communities Fund?

Yes. The Plateau Fund has a separate review process, and applying to it does not affect Community Impact eligibility.
