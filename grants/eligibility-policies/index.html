Grants

Eligibility & Grant Policies

General requirements that apply to all competitive grant programs. Each program page lists additional eligibility criteria.

Who may apply

  • Organizations recognized as tax-exempt under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code
  • Public and governmental agencies, including public schools, libraries, and tribal colleges
  • Federally recognized tribes, tribal programs, Navajo Nation chapters, and Hopi villages
  • Projects with a qualified fiscal sponsor that is a 501(c)(3) organization
  • Fundraising events, sponsorships, and advertising

Geographic eligibility

Most programs serve Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, and Apache counties. Three community funds accept applications from anywhere in Arizona.

ProgramGeographic eligibility
Community Impact Grants: Spring CycleOrganizations must be located in or provide services to residents of Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, or Apache counties, Arizona.
Community Impact Grants: Fall CycleOrganizations must be located in or provide services to residents of Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, or Apache counties, Arizona.
First Steps Infant & Toddler FundApplicants must serve families who live in Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, or Apache counties. Priority is given to proposals serving Apache and Navajo counties and rural areas of Coconino County.
Plateau Indigenous Communities FundApplications are accepted from across Arizona. Priority is given to work on or near the Navajo Nation, Hopi Reservation, and other tribal lands in northern Arizona, and to urban Native communities in Flagstaff and Prescott.
Prism Fund for LGBTQ+ ArizonansApplications are accepted from organizations serving communities anywhere in Arizona. Proposals serving rural counties receive additional consideration.
Rising Roots Black Futures FundApplications are accepted from organizations serving communities anywhere in Arizona.
Stronger Nonprofits Capacity GrantsOrganizations must be headquartered in Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, or Apache counties.
High Country Rapid Response & Resilience FundOrganizations must be responding to emergencies affecting residents of Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, or Apache counties, including tribal lands within those counties.
Shared Giving CatalogOrganizations must be located in or primarily serve residents of Coconino, Yavapai, Navajo, or Apache counties.

Application limits

  • Organizations may submit one Community Impact Grant application per calendar year, in either the Spring or Fall cycle.
  • Organizations that received a Community Impact Grant in 2026 may next apply in 2028. They remain eligible for the community funds, Capacity Grants, the Rapid Response Fund, and the Shared Giving Catalog.
  • Applying to a community fund (First Steps, Plateau, Prism, or Rising Roots) does not affect Community Impact eligibility.

What we do not fund

  • Grants to individuals, scholarships, or private school tuition (scholarships are offered through separate donor funds)
  • Religious activities, although faith-based organizations providing secular services open to all are eligible
  • Capital campaigns, endowments, or debt reduction
  • Lobbying or partisan political activity

Nondiscrimination

The Foundation makes grants without regard to race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, tribal affiliation, age, disability, or veteran status. Grantees must not discriminate in hiring, board membership, or the delivery of services.

Fiscal sponsorship

Projects without their own 501(c)(3) status may apply through a fiscal sponsor. Submit a signed letter using the Fiscal Sponsor Letter Template. Award tiers for general operating grants are based on the project's budget.

Conflicts of interest

Board members, committee members, and staff who are affiliated with an applicant do not participate in reviewing or voting on that application.
